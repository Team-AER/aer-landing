The most sensitive conversations in business now happen in front of a recorder nobody in the room controls.

Muffliato is an audio layer that keeps speech readable to people and unreadable to transcription models. The technology works today; we are a pre-incorporation company raising a pre-seed round to turn it into a product with paying customers in legal, advisory and corporate settings.

In thirty months, unconsented transcription went from a complaint to a liability with a budget.

Courts, bar associations, universities and the meeting platforms themselves now treat an uninvited transcript as something to block. Budget follows liability, and the budget sits with the people who carry it: general counsel, CISOs and company secretaries.

What we built: an adversarial layer in the audio itself.

The same toolbox as forensic watermarking, with the opposite goal. A watermark is built so a model still hears every word and a detector can find the leaker afterwards. Muffliato is built so the model hears something else, before a copy exists.

Imperceptible

A learned adversarial perturbation shaped to the speech, below what listeners notice.

Survives distribution

Designed to hold through compression, re-encoding and re-recording, the same conditions watermarks meet.

Yours to apply

Applied live in the meeting pipeline, or once at export for files. No platform cooperation needed, no detector to run later.

The buyer is whoever carries the liability, and each one already has a budget line this sits beside.

  1. 1. Land: the sensitive seats

    A deal team, a litigation group, a board secretariat: 20 to 200 seats bought on one incident or one bar opinion. Priced per protected seat, per month.

  2. 2. Expand: every room that records

    A firm-wide licence with policy controls and audit logs, through Zoom and Teams admin. The clients of the first law firms become the next deal teams and boards.

  3. 3. Later: files, not only calls

    Recordings, depositions and pre-release media protected per copy through the platforms that already watermark them; voice talent and publishers as a parallel market.

Against bot blocking and consent banners, which only cover the recorder you control, and forensic watermarking, which identifies a leaker after the leak, Muffliato is the only control that acts on the speech itself. Nobody else in the category has raised a round.

What would make us change course

If twenty interviews find every buyer satisfied by bot blocking plus consent banners, and none can name a recorder outside their control, the meetings market is not there yet. The same technology then moves to per-file protection of recordings and pre-release media, where platforms already pay per copy.
