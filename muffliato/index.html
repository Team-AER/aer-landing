For recordings you can’t stop

Bot blocking and consent cover most meetings. Muffliato covers the rest.

  • First

    Confidential meetings

    Law, health, boards, universities. The other side records anyway.

  • Second

    Voice talent and publishers

    Narrators and podcasts. Keep published audio out of training sets.

  • Third

    Pre-release review copies

    Screeners and dailies. Already watermarked; now unreadable to machines.

  • Fourth

    Interviews and sources

    Raw footage shared with freelancers and funders. Protect the source.

Why this, why now

Buyers are already fighting machine transcription. Nothing they have acts on the audio itself.

  1. Aug 2025

    Otter.ai sued over meeting audio. Universities start blocking AI notetakers.

  2. Dec 2025

    NYC Bar requires client consent for AI recording. A patient sues over an ambient scribe.

  3. Mar 2026

    VCU approves zero third-party notetakers. Granola raises $125M to build one.

  4. May 2026

    Nine class actions by journalists and narrators over AI use of their voices.

  5. Aug 2026

    Wiretap claims against Otter.ai survive dismissal. Zoom ships bot blocking.

  6. Oct 2026

    3.2 million podcast episodes indexed with full transcripts. No one objects.

What happens to a recording

It works on the audio itself, before the file leaves your hands.

  1. 1

    You hand it a recording

    Any finished audio file. No transcript or speaker details needed.

  2. 2

    It adds a quiet layer

    Far too quiet to notice, shaped to confuse transcription software and nothing else.

  3. 3

    You get the same recording back

    Same length, same format, same words. Transcription returns empty.
