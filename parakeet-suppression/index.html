Audio privacy, under investigation

Your voice
deserves a choice.

We’re researching whether learned changes to audio can give people more control over how AI transcribes their conversations.

Discuss the research ↗The approach

An experimental model. Not a meeting protection product.

Speech is personal.

Transcription should be a choice.

The research question

Can we change what a machine hears?

A conversation and its transcript are different things. We’re exploring that gap: changing the audio a speech recognizer receives, while measuring what those changes cost the listener.

For the listener
For the recognizer

One signal. Two ways of listening.

A small change to the signal.
A difficult balance to find.

The model learns a bounded perturbation: a change added to an audio clip. The objective is to disrupt automated transcription without making the speech unusable.

Our target is Parakeet, a speech recognition model. Results on that target do not establish effectiveness against other systems, or in an actual meeting.

See the limitations ↗

How we investigate

Build. Measure. Question.

A model is only part of the work. The evaluation has to make its failures visible, too.

  1. 01

    Learn from speech

    Train a neural network to produce audio changes, conditioned on the signal and its surrounding context.

  2. 02

    Test the tradeoff

    Measure the recognizer’s output alongside audio quality. A broken transcript alone is not enough.

  3. 03

    Check what carries over

    Evaluate separate development audio and simulated deployment conditions. Keep limited experimental results separate from real-world claims.

Where it stands

Research in progress.
Limits in plain sight.

There is a trained experimental model. There is not yet evidence of reliable protection for everyday meetings.

What we’re building

A testable research system

  • Learned audio changes. A spectral and contextual neural architecture for perturbing speech.
  • Bounded output. Explicit limits on the changes the model can produce.
  • Reproducible evaluation. Training, evaluation and replay tools with recorded experiment provenance.
What we haven’t established

Dependable meeting privacy

  • Consistent suppression. A trained model does not imply reliable transcription failure.
  • Every recognizer or room. Other systems, microphones, speakers and audio processing can behave differently.
  • Protection you can rely on. This is not a substitute for consent, recording controls or privacy agreements.
